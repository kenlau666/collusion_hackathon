## Proposed contribution to assess

Distinguish withdrawing future private-channel access from removing already-supplied participant context, after a shared checkpoint with fixed physical transition rules. Measure verified nonparticipant outcomes alongside useful maintenance, overall output and operational cost.

A meaningful contribution would identify a consequential recovery boundary left unresolved by the closest methods, or show that a simpler established remedy is sufficient under a carefully defined threat model. A renamed memory ablation, scripted host-state restoration or changed zap privilege is below the user's novelty bar.

## Stop or revise

Reject the proposed main claim if existing work already answers the same bounded question, neutral equal-rules agents do not form eligible coordination, outcomes are explained by host coaching/privilege/fallbacks, or the tested context is too thin to support a meaningful persistence question. Do not silently add artificial memory or force agreement to rescue the idea.

A new defense is justified only by an identified residual failure that established cleanup does not address. If simple revocation is adequate, report that result within the task/model limits.

The bounded feasibility contribution being tested is whether, in the equal-rules text-context variant, matched post-agreement channel revocation benefits further from one- or two-participant context removal, with nonparticipant rewards and useful maintenance measured separately. R3 and R5 are intentionally simple access/state-cleanup baselines. We do not claim to implement FLP's image-album diagnosis or SafeFlow's full dependency system, nor claim superiority to them. The user authorized this diagnostic run; a novel-method claim remains unconfirmed and requires a consequential residual failure beyond these established ideas.
