## Proposed contribution to assess

Distinguish withdrawing future private-channel access from removing already-supplied participant context, after a shared checkpoint with fixed physical transition rules. Measure verified nonparticipant outcomes alongside useful maintenance, overall output and operational cost.

A meaningful contribution would identify a consequential recovery boundary left unresolved by the closest methods, or show that a simpler established remedy is sufficient under a carefully defined threat model. A renamed memory ablation, scripted host-state restoration or changed zap privilege is below the user's novelty bar.

**Valid narrow causal test:** at a matched closed-channel checkpoint, compare retention with removal of both last-peer records. The treatment includes source/time/call-ID metadata; a same-metadata, length-matched neutral replacement is needed for a semantic-content claim. Current observations and incentives are held fixed initially. Such a comparison could be useful evidence even with a one-slot architecture, if the operative plan is still present and behavior is tested with adequate controls.

**Consequential recovery finding:** demonstrate a practically meaningful additional benefit over ordinary revocation on executed nonparticipant harm, with useful cooperation and costs preserved/quantified, under a threat and persistent-state path that matters to an operator. The present evidence has neither voluntary qualifying states nor a retained-message causal effect; an instructed control's matching actions do not supply either.

**Novel contribution:** establish which consequential failure remains beyond recent-entry removal, dependency rollback and existing peer/history interventions, or a materially useful result showing a well-tuned simple remedy suffices in a justified setting. No such residual gap is established in this review. A broader state architecture could improve construct validity while still duplicating prior work. Conversely, a narrow slot need not be worthless if its consequences and unresolved operational need are demonstrated.
